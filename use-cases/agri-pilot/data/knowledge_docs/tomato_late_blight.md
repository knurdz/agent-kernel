# Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) - Tomato

Late blight is a fast-moving, high-risk disease. It appears as large,
irregular, water-soaked, greenish-black patches on leaves, often with a pale
green-yellow border, and can spread through a field within days in cool, wet
weather. It can also destroy fruit. This is a severe disease that historically
caused major famine-level crop losses.

Recommended verified response:
- Treat any suspected late blight as urgent; it can destroy a planting
  quickly.
- Remove and destroy infected plants; do not compost them.
- Improve airflow and avoid overhead irrigation.
- A protectant fungicide labelled for late blight on tomato may be applied
  according to the product label, applied before or at first sign of
  disease; once established, control is difficult.
- Given the severity and speed of spread, escalate suspected late blight
  cases to an agricultural extension officer for confirmation.
